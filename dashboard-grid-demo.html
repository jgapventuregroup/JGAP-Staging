Deal Analyzer

Check whether a property could meet your income goals using your own estimates.

  1. Enter the property income, costs and loan terms below. Replace the example numbers when starting a manual calculation.
  2. Select Analyze offer to see the estimated results.
  3. For a saved property, select Save changes to this property to keep your updated numbers. Analyzing alone does not save changes.
What do these numbers mean?
Asking price and your offer
Asking price is what the seller wants. Your offer is the price you are considering; it can be different.
Vacancy
The share of potential rent you expect to lose when units are empty or rent is not collected. For example, 5% of $2,000 is $100 per month. Do not include the same allowance again in operating expenses.
Monthly operating expenses
Include property taxes, insurance, repairs, management, owner-paid utilities and any regular reserves you choose to budget. Use monthly amounts; divide annual bills by 12. Keep mortgage payments separate because the calculator estimates them from the loan terms.
Down payment and closing/repair costs
The down payment is your upfront share of the purchase price. Closing and repair costs are additional upfront amounts, entered in dollars.
Monthly cash flow
Estimated income left after the vacancy allowance, operating expenses and the primary mortgage payment. Additional CELCO/HELOC borrowing is shown separately when used. This is an estimate based on your inputs.
Cash-on-cash return
Estimated annual cash flow divided by the upfront cash used in the calculation, shown as a percentage. The maximum-offer tool also includes the additional cash reserves entered there. This is different from property appreciation or a guaranteed return.
